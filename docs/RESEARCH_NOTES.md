# Research notes

TieSua addresses access to algebra when visual notation is unavailable or difficult to read. Its design supports incremental practice, explicit mathematical grouping, and feedback tied to verified steps. These are design choices. Their effect on learning has not been established here.

Proposed studies could measure spoken grouping comprehension, error recovery, interaction latency and independent completion. Compare responses using synthetic tasks before recruiting. Establish consent, accessible study materials, ethics approval and data handling with the institution before collecting identifiable learner data. Keep protocols and empirical findings distinct from proposals.

## Candidate research designs

- **Spoken grouping comprehension.** Present matched expressions with and without structural cues. Ask participants to reconstruct what is divided or predict a value after substitution. Counterbalance the order. Track grouping accuracy separately from calculation accuracy and control the voice and speech rate.
- **Recovery after a mistake.** Compare an explicit retry with a short hint tied to the learner's verified error. Measure whether the learner can explain the correction and apply it to a new problem. Record whether recognition or mathematics caused each error.
- **Hands-free pacing.** Compare silence settings, explicit interruption and a physically validated interruption pilot. Measure missed utterances, accidental interruptions and how often assistance is needed. Device and screen reader choice can confound the comparison.

These are study proposals. The repository's automated tests, synthetic benchmark and owner observations are engineering evidence. Participant recruitment, instruments, handling of identifiable data and the interpretation of learning outcomes require a separate approved protocol.
